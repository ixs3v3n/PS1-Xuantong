\textbf{Mechanism.} Buyers face scarce goods under two rules: refundable/non-transferable versus transferable/non-refundable. Agents differ in income uncertainty and speculative participation. Reputation signals vary in reliability and visibility. Outcomes are purchase probability, surplus, speculation, and access by buyer type. The simulation uses a fixed seed and repeated rounds; the focused test fixes No History and Low income uncertainty before comparing the two rules.

\textbf{Focused test.} The main comparison uses 100 buyers, 30 goods, 200 rounds per condition, random seed 42, No History, and Low income uncertainty. Genuine access rate is genuine winners divided by genuine buyers; speculation rate is speculator winners divided by all winners; transaction rate is all winners divided by buyers. The saved baseline outputs are 42.38\% genuine access, 0\% speculation, and 29.63\% transaction rate for refundable/non-transferable, versus 1.93\%, 95.17\%, and 29.95\% for transferable/non-refundable.

\textbf{Compact rule.} For each treatment: initialize heterogeneous buyers and scarce supply $\rightarrow$ generate bids from buyer type, reputation, income uncertainty, and platform rule $\rightarrow$ generate the market asking price $\rightarrow$ allocate up to 30 goods to eligible highest bidders $\rightarrow$ record transaction, genuine access, speculation, and surplus $\rightarrow$ repeat for 200 rounds.

\textbf{Checks.} Boundary: zero speculative buyers and minimum demand are conceptual boundary cases. Typical: baseline demand, scarcity, and mixed buyer types. Invalid input: negative demand or an income probability outside $[0,1]$; these are documented model boundaries, although the current notebook does not expose a user-input validation interface. The computational notebook additionally checks robustness at 10\%, 30\%, and 50\% speculator shares. Results from the Week 2 browser prototype are reported separately from the PS1 simulation.

\textbf{Responsible boundary.} The learning demo uses a static browser implementation only: no Hugging Face CPU/GPU runtime, backend, database, secret, API call, or paid service. Checks include desktop/mobile layout, keyboard interaction, readable labels, reset behavior, and invalid-input handling. Privacy is not collected or stored. Population-level behavioral claims remain unverified until larger experiments are conducted.

\section{Review response and revision record}
\textbf{Strength retained:} Peer review confirmed that the project clearly connects platform rules, reputation information, and income uncertainty across economics, computation, and behavioral science. I retain this interdisciplinary structure.\\
\textbf{Constructive concern addressed:} Peer review requested clearer, checkable evidence. I added a focused baseline test with fixed reputation and income uncertainty and report exact simulation parameters and outcomes.\\
\textbf{Question clarified:} The question about distinguishing reduced speculation from reduced genuine participation led me to separate speculation rate, genuine-consumer access rate, and overall transaction rate.\\
\textbf{Revision consequence:} I narrowed the computational claim to a testable comparison of transferable/non-refundable versus refundable/non-transferable rules. I retain broader reputation and uncertainty conditions as secondary analysis rather than presenting them as the main causal test.
